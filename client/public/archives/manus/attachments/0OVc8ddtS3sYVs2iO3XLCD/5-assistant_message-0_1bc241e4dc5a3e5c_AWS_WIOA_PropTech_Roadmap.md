## 3. Scoring Methodology

Each program is scored on five weighted criteria aligned to your stated goals: a $30+/hour remote job in the near term, and building an AI-powered PropTech company in Puerto Rico in the medium term.

| Criterion | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Credential value | 25% | Does it produce an industry-recognized AWS certification, and how much do employers value it? |
| WIOA fit | 20% | Approvability, cost-to-outcome ratio, structure, and completion likelihood |
| Employment impact | 20% | Realistic effect on landing a $30+/hr remote role within 6–12 months |
| PropTech startup value | 20% | Direct applicability to architecting, building, and automating your rental platform |
| Sequencing fit | 15% | Is this the right course *now*, given your current skill level? |

Scores are grounded in verified program facts and cited market data rather than intuition. A program can be excellent in absolute terms but score poorly here if it is mistimed (e.g., MLOps before you know AWS fundamentals) or structurally weak as a WIOA request (e.g., a 2-day seminar with no credential).

### Why the SCSU sequence wins decisively

The SCSU sequence is not merely "a good foundation" — it is structurally the only option on pages 1–2 of your list that satisfies every WIOA priority at once. It is explicitly WIOA-approved per SCSU's own site, it runs long enough (18–20 weeks of live Tuesday/Thursday evening classes) to count as substantive training, and it culminates in **two** industry-recognized credentials whose exam fees ($100 for CCP, $150 for SAA) are already bundled into the $1,250 price [1]. The SAA is consistently ranked the single most valuable entry credential in cloud computing — KodeKloud's 2026 analysis calls it "the certification that gets the most interviews, period" [11] — and SAA holders average roughly $127,000 per year in Payscale's cert-attributed data [7], with Skillsoft's US survey figure at $155,597 for (typically experienced) holders [8]. Even discounting heavily for your entry-level status, entry cloud roles commonly post at $63,000–$90,000 [12], comfortably clearing your $30/hour ($62,400/year) target.

Just as important for your startup: the SAA curriculum — VPC networking, IAM security, RDS/DynamoDB databases, S3 storage, Lambda, API Gateway, auto-scaling, monitoring — is a nearly one-to-one match with the infrastructure your PropTech platform requires. ChatGPT's instinct here was right; it simply couldn't prove it.

### Why the expensive short courses fall

This is the largest correction to the earlier analysis. *Developing on AWS*, *Developing Generative AI Applications on AWS*, and *MLOps Engineering on AWS* are official AWS classroom seminars — genuinely high quality — but they run only two to three days, include no certification exam, and assume prerequisites you do not yet have (the GenAI course expects working Python and prior AWS development experience [2]; MLOps expects ML and DevOps background [3]). Spending $1,390–$2,099 of a capped WIOA account on a 2–3 day class with no credential, before you hold any AWS certification, would be the single worst allocation available on your list. These courses belong in Phase 3 of your journey, ideally self-funded or employer-funded once you are working.

### Where the Udacity and budget options fit

The CT State Gateway *AWS Cloud Foundations* course ($550, 4 weeks) is a sensible fallback **only if** your counselor declines the SCSU sequence — it covers just the CCP level and includes only a discount voucher, not the exam. The Udacity nanodegrees produce excellent portfolio projects but only Udacity completion certificates, are self-paced (a real completion risk), and don't include exam fees — making them weaker WIOA requests than instructor-led, credential-bearing programs, though their project work is worth emulating on your own.

## 5. Reality Check: What Actually Gets You Hired

A certification opens the door to interviews; it does not, by itself, produce a job offer. Community and industry consensus is blunt on this point: employers want demonstrable, hands-on ability [9]. This is normally the hard part for career changers — but you have an unfair advantage. **Your PropTech platform is your portfolio.** Every AWS concept from the SCSU sequence should be immediately applied to a real, deployed component of your rental platform: Cognito for tenant/landlord authentication, S3 for document storage, Lambda + API Gateway for maintenance-request workflows, Bedrock for AI listing generation. By exam day, you will have both the credential and a live production system to show interviewers — the exact combination that separates hired candidates from certificate collectors.

The remote market supports your target. Cloud engineer postings in Puerto Rico average approximately $61.66/hour in San Juan per ZipRecruiter [13], Indeed lists hundreds of cloud roles in PR with ranges of $93,200–$164,450 [12], and fully remote mainland roles widen the pool further. Your $30/hour floor is a conservative, achievable first step, not a stretch goal.

## 6. Your Phased Roadmap

### Phase 1 — Now: Request the SCSU sequence from WIOA
Ask your Workforce Alliance counselor to fund the **SCSU AWS Cloud Practitioner + Solutions Architect Sequence ($1,250)**. Talking points for your counselor: it is explicitly WIOA-approved, leads to two industry-recognized credentials with exam fees included, is instructor-led (higher completion rates than self-paced), maps to in-demand occupations (cloud engineer, solutions architect), and costs well under the ITA cap — leaving headroom if local policy permits a follow-on course. During these 18–20 weeks, build your PropTech MVP in parallel using the AWS Free Tier.

### Phase 2 — Months 5–9: Developer skills + job search
After passing the SAA, self-study for the **AWS Certified Developer – Associate** ($150 exam) using AWS Skill Builder (much of it free) and low-cost prep courses (~$15–30), rather than the $2,095 *Developing on AWS* seminar — the exam is the credential; the seminar is not. Simultaneously, apply for remote cloud/support roles with your SAA + live platform portfolio. Income at this stage funds everything after.

### Phase 3 — Months 9–18: AI specialization
Once employed and with your platform running, pursue the AI layer: the **AWS Certified AI Practitioner** and/or **Machine Learning Engineer – Associate** certifications, which are among the fastest-growing credentials for 2026 [11]. If your employer or a second WIOA allocation can fund it, *Developing Generative AI Applications on AWS* (Bedrock) becomes genuinely valuable **at this stage** — you will finally have the prerequisites to absorb it, and it maps directly to your AI features (lease review, applicant screening, contractor matching).

### Phase 4 — Scale: Puerto Rico advantage
As you relocate the business to Puerto Rico, investigate **Act 60** export-services tax incentives for your technology company, and consider the *Microservices at Scale using AWS and Kubernetes* material when your platform outgrows a single application architecture.

## 7. What to Do With the Remaining 64 Pages of the CTHires List

As you review the remaining pages, apply this five-question filter before sending anything for analysis; it will eliminate roughly 80% of listings immediately:

1. **Does it lead to an industry-recognized certification with the exam included or clearly attainable?** If no, deprioritize.
2. **Is it at least 8 weeks and instructor-led?** Multi-week structured programs beat 2–3 day seminars and hours-long modules for WIOA value.
3. **Is it appropriate for your current level?** Anything requiring existing AWS, Python, or ML experience belongs in Phase 3, not Phase 1.
4. **Is the cost proportionate to the outcome?** A $2,000 program should deliver dramatically more than a $1,250 one; on your list so far, none does.
5. **Does it serve both goals?** Prefer programs that advance employment *and* your PropTech build simultaneously.

Programs worth watching for on later pages: any **AWS AI Practitioner** or **ML Engineer – Associate** prep with exam included, longer-form **software development bootcamps** with credentials, and **CompTIA Security+** style programs if you want a security credential to complement the architecture path.
