## Detailed Findings

### 1. Phone Etiquette and Call Handling

Phone etiquette was designated the highest-priority area. The PropertyMax dashboard demonstrated that the platform can retain audio, AI summaries, sentiment labels, follow-up tasks, call categories, and a Golden Score. On July 14, the property dashboard displayed 12 calls and an 83% Marketing Golden Score.[3]

However, the visible call records were associated with Kimberly Powell, not Kristina Marshall. The external Manager Phone Portal returned an empty login shell in the secure session, and the PropertyMax Call Activity report did not load before the expedited cutoff.[4] No Kristina-specific recordings, call dispositions, missed-call counts, callback times, escalation history, resident complaints, or six-month quality trend were available.

Accordingly, the audit found **no substantiated evidence of either poor or strong phone etiquette by Kristina**. The 83% property score must not be attributed to her. Any employment or coaching decision about phone conduct should follow a direct, documented call-quality review.

### 2. Leasing and Marketing

The marketing report recorded ten referral entries across five sources, led by Facebook and Apartments.com.[1] This establishes that referral-source tracking existed, but it does not establish whether Kristina answered inquiries promptly, qualified prospects accurately, scheduled tours, completed follow-ups, converted leads, or documented lost prospects.

The July 14 call snapshot contained property-level inquiries about application documents, unit availability, pricing, holding fees, application status, and office visits. Those visible interactions were associated with Kimberly Powell. They demonstrate the types of leasing questions received at Windsor/Yorkshire, not Kristina’s individual performance.[3]

The leasing conclusion is therefore **limited assurance**. A defensible rating requires a Kristina-specific guest-card funnel showing inquiry ownership, first-response time, contact attempts, tours, applications, approvals, leases, and documented loss reasons.

### 3. Collections

No verified six-month collection report, resident ledger sample, delinquency aging, payment-plan log, promise-to-pay record, notice history, or collection communication set was obtained. The audit therefore cannot assess Kristina’s follow-up cadence, documentation quality, accuracy, professionalism, escalation, or effect on delinquency.

The absence of reviewed records is an audit limitation, not evidence of deficient collections performance. A supplemental review should reconcile the PropertyMax Collected Report or source accounting system to resident notes and notices attributed to Kristina.

### 4. Narratives and Management Reporting

The six-month search returned seven narrative submissions on six unique dates. Every record identified Kimberly Powell/K. Powell as the Property Manager; none was attributed to Kristina.[2] Consequently, the narratives cannot be used to assess Kristina’s individual reporting quality or management follow-through.

Two June 26 entries repeated overtime and leasing figures but conflicted on evictions, showing 0 in one record and 7 in the other.[2] This exception creates uncertainty about the reliability of property reporting and should be reconciled to the eviction or legal-status source record. The control issue is material because narrative data may influence staffing, leasing, legal, and operational decisions.

### 5. Resident Communications

The available call snapshot showed that the property received detailed leasing and resident-facing inquiries and that follow-up tasks could be generated in PropertyMax.[3] The record did not show Kristina as the call handler. No Kristina-specific SMS sample, complaint log, escalation set, or callback record was reviewed.

The audit therefore cannot rate Kristina’s professionalism, empathy, accuracy, responsiveness, or follow-through in resident communications. The appropriate next step is a sample-based quality review rather than an inference from property-level volume.

### 6. Compliance

No six-month compliance checklist, fair-housing review, notice audit, inspection exception file, accommodation-request log, legal-deadline tracker, or policy-acknowledgment record was verified. The narratives’ conflicting eviction counts further increase the importance of reconciling legal and notice-related data, but they do not establish that Kristina caused the discrepancy.

No adverse compliance conclusion about Kristina is supported by the current evidence. Compliance should remain **unrated** until source records are reviewed and individual responsibility is established.

### 7. Work Orders

Separate work-order-system access was deferred at the requester’s direction. The report therefore does not evaluate work-order aging, emergency response, resident updates, vendor coordination, completion quality, repeat orders, or Kristina’s maintenance follow-through.

## Recommended Corrective Action Plan

### Immediate: Within Five Business Days

Management should reconcile the two June 26 narrative entries, confirm the correct eviction count, and document which record is authoritative. Weekly narratives should require a verified author, property, reporting period, and submission timestamp. Property-level metrics should not be presented as Kristina’s individual results unless the system records her ownership.

### Phone Etiquette Review: Within Ten Business Days

A reviewer should select a **stratified random sample of at least 20 Kristina-specific calls** across the six-month period, including leasing, rent, urgent, maintenance, inbound, and outbound calls where available. Each call should be scored consistently for greeting and identification, tone and respect, active listening, accuracy, policy compliance, ownership, hold/transfer handling, clear next steps, documentation, and professional closing. Missed calls and voicemails should be tested for callback timeliness. Any complaint should be corroborated against the recording and follow-up record.

### Operational Completion: Within Fifteen Business Days

Management should extract a Kristina-specific leasing funnel, collections history, resident communication sample, and compliance exception file. The supplemental review should compare documented expectations with actual ownership and outcomes. Work-order performance should be added only if the separate system is authorized for review.

## Final Management Conclusion

The current audit does **not** substantiate misconduct, poor phone etiquette, weak collections, compliance failure, or poor overall performance by Kristina Marshall. It also does not substantiate consistently strong performance in those areas. The evidence is insufficient and frequently attributable to another manager.

The defensible management action is to correct the record-governance weaknesses and complete a targeted, Kristina-specific supplemental review. **No adverse employment decision should be based solely on the present report.**
