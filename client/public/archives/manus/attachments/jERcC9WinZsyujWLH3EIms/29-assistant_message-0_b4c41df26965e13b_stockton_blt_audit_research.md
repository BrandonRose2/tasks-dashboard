# How the City of Stockton Evaluates and Verifies Business License Tax Applications

**Prepared by Manus AI | March 31, 2026**

---

## Overview

The City of Stockton operates a self-reporting business license tax (BLT) system, meaning applicants are trusted to declare their own gross receipts and other taxable metrics. However, this self-reported information is **explicitly not treated as conclusive** under the Municipal Code. The City maintains a layered framework of administrative verification, audit authority, and civil and criminal enforcement mechanisms to ensure compliance. The following report synthesizes the relevant provisions from Stockton's Municipal Code (Title 5) and publicly available financial records.

---

## 1. The Self-Reporting Framework and Its Legal Limits

When a business submits its BLT application, the applicant signs the gross receipts declaration **under penalty of perjury** — the application must be "written under penalty of perjury or sworn to by the applicant before a person authorized to administer oaths." [^1] For a first-time license, the applicant submits an *estimate* of expected gross receipts; within 30 days of the license period expiring, they must then file a sworn reconciliation statement showing actual gross receipts, with any underpayment corrected at that time. [^2]

The Municipal Code is unambiguous that these sworn statements are a starting point, not the final word:

> "No statement shall be conclusive as to the matters set forth therein, nor shall the filing of the same preclude the City from collecting by appropriate action such sum as is actually due and payable hereunder." [^3]

This means the City retains the right to independently verify any figure reported on an application, regardless of whether the applicant has signed it under oath.

---

## 2. The City's Audit Authority

### 2.1 Director of Finance — Book Inspection Rights

The primary audit authority rests with the **Director of Finance** (or a designated member of their department). Under §5.04.180, the Director has the explicit right to:

> "inspect the books of any person engaged in or carrying on any profession, trade, calling, occupation or business within the City for the purpose of ascertaining the true license tax of such person." [^3]

This right is exercisable after providing **five days' written notice** to the business. All licensees, applicants, and persons engaged in business are legally required to permit such an examination. [^4] The scope of an examination can include any books and records the Director deems necessary to determine the correct tax owed.

A parallel provision in Title 3 (Revenue and Finance) reinforces this, stating that the Director of Finance "is hereby authorized to examine, audit and inspect such books and records of any licensee or applicant for license as may be necessary in his or her judgment to ascertain the correct amount of license due." [^5]

### 2.2 What Triggers a Closer Look

While the City does not publish a formal "audit trigger" list, the Municipal Code identifies several specific circumstances that can prompt the Director of Finance to independently determine the tax owed:

| Trigger | Relevant Code Section |
|---|---|
| Failure to file a required gross receipts statement | §5.04.200(A) |
| Failure to file a *corrected* statement within 15 days of demand | §5.04.200(A) |
| Filed statement that "does not set forth the true facts" | §5.04.200(A) |
| Failure to file any statement at all | §3.12.070 / §3.12.080 |

When any of these triggers apply, the Director may estimate the tax owed using "such information as it may be able to obtain," including external indices such as dividends paid, sales or use taxes paid, or other comparable business data. [^6]

### 2.3 The FY 2023–24 Gross Receipts Clean-Up

The City's own **FY 2023–24 Annual Comprehensive Financial Report (ACFR)** confirms that active compliance work is ongoing. The report specifically notes "gross receipt reporting and a clean-up of business license data resulting in more accurate and timely billing of business license accounts." [^7] This indicates the City's Finance Department periodically reconciles its business license database against reported gross receipts and pursues corrections — a practical, data-driven form of audit activity that goes beyond passive acceptance of self-reported figures.

## 3. Confidentiality of Audit Information

Information gathered through examination or audit is **strictly confidential**. Under §5.04.190:

> "The information furnished or secured pursuant to the provisions of this chapter through Chapter 5.20 shall be deemed confidential in character, and shall not be subject to public inspection and shall be kept so that the contents thereof shall not become known except to the persons charged with the administration of this chapter." [^8]

The Director of Finance and all agents and employees are prohibited from divulging facts or information obtained during administration, unless compelled by a judicial order or required for proper administration of the code. This means that audit findings about a specific business are not made public.

## 4. Penalties and Enforcement

The City's enforcement framework operates on several escalating levels, summarized in the table below.

| Violation | Penalty | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Operating without a license | 25% of license fee due, plus daily violations | §5.04.040(B) |
| Delinquent payment (first month) | +15% of tax due | §5.04.070(B) |
| Each additional month delinquent | +15% per month, capped at 60% total | §5.04.070(B) |
| Operating without a license (additional) | Up to 60% penalty (15%/month × 4 months max) | §5.04.070(B) |
| Failure to file a required statement | City determines tax by estimate; guilty of a code violation | §5.04.180 |
| False statement on application | Criminal violation (misdemeanor); punishable under City code | §5.04.180 / §5.04.150 |

### 4.1 The 25% "Unlicensed Operation" Penalty

This is particularly relevant for Granite Ridge Housing LP, which appears to have been operating without a license for a period of time. Any business that carries on operations without first obtaining a license sustains **an automatic 25% penalty** on top of all taxes owed, in addition to the standard delinquency penalties. [^9] Each day of unlicensed operation constitutes a separate violation.

### 4.2 Delinquency Penalty Structure

Once a license tax becomes delinquent, penalties accrue at **15% of the amount due per month**, up to a maximum of **60% of the original tax**. [^10] This is the penalty structure already reflected in the application's Prior Years worksheet — the 15%/month up to 60% cap, plus the additional 25% unlicensed-operation penalty, together produce the total penalties shown in the Tax Summary panel.

### 4.3 Criminal Exposure for False Statements

Applications are signed under penalty of perjury. A false statement in a BLT application can constitute a criminal violation under the City code, and potentially under California Penal Code §532a(1) (false financial statements), which carries misdemeanor penalties of up to six months in jail and fines. The City code itself states that failure to file a required statement makes a person "guilty of a violation hereof, and be punishable therefor as provided by the code of the City." [^3]

## 5. The Appeals Process

If the Director of Finance issues an assessment after an audit or estimated determination, the business has the right to appeal:

1. **Administrative Hearing**: Within 10 days of receiving the assessment notice, the business may request a hearing before the Director of Finance. [^6]
2. **City Council Appeal**: If still aggrieved after the Director's determination, the business may appeal to the City Council by filing a notice of appeal with the Director of Finance within 15 days of the decision. The Council's findings are final and conclusive. [^11]

## 6. Broader Context: State-Level Oversight

In December 2025, the **California State Controller's Office** launched an audit of the City of Stockton's overall finances to examine how public funds are managed and whether taxpayer money is being used appropriately. [^12] While this is a city-wide financial audit rather than a business license audit, it reflects a broader environment of increased scrutiny of Stockton's financial operations. A March 2026 audit by an independent firm found widespread internal control weaknesses in the City's financial operations. [^13] This context suggests the City's Finance Department is under pressure to tighten compliance across all revenue streams, including business license taxes.

## Key Takeaways for Granite Ridge Housing LP

The following points are most directly relevant to your application:

**The application is not self-certifying.** The City can and does audit gross receipts figures. Ensure that the income figures reported in the Prior Years worksheet are consistent with your tax returns and financial statements, as the Director of Finance can request to inspect those records with five days' notice.

**Penalties are already accruing.** The application reflects penalties for operating without a license. Submitting the application promptly and paying the assessed amount stops further penalty accrual.

**Confidentiality is protected.** The gross receipts figures you report are confidential and cannot be disclosed publicly by City staff, except under court order.

**An appeal path exists.** If you believe the City's assessment is incorrect, you have a structured right to request a hearing and, if necessary, appeal to the City Council.
