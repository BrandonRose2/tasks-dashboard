# Diagnostic Report: 2018 Ford Raptor "Low Engine Oil Pressure" Warning

Based on the video provided and extensive research into the 2018 Ford F-150 Raptor (3.5L EcoBoost engine), this report outlines the potential causes for the persistent "Low Engine Oil Pressure" warning you are experiencing, especially after a mechanic has verified the oil levels and reset the computer. It also includes several DIY steps you can take before returning to a mechanic.

## Video Analysis Summary

The video shows the vehicle traveling at approximately 20 mph in Drive (D). The odometer reads 140,114 miles. At the 5-second mark, a red warning box appears on the center digital display reading **"Low Engine Oil Pressure"** accompanied by an oil can icon and an audible chime. Notably, the oil pressure gauge on the left side of the cluster drops to zero when this warning appears.

## Potential Causes

The 3.5L EcoBoost engine in the 2018 Ford Raptor has several known issues that can trigger this specific warning, ranging from simple sensor failures to more complex mechanical problems.

### 1. Faulty Oil Pressure Sensor/Switch (Most Common)
The most frequent cause of this warning on the 3.5L EcoBoost is a failing oil pressure sensor (also known as an oil pressure switch) [1]. Even if the computer is reset, a faulty sensor will continue to send incorrect zero-pressure readings to the dashboard, triggering the warning and sometimes putting the truck into "limp mode" (restricting RPMs to protect the engine) [2]. 

### 2. Contaminated Sensor Wiring Harness
Oil leaks from the engine can drip down and contaminate the electrical connector that plugs into the oil pressure sensor [2]. When oil gets inside this connector, it disrupts the electrical signal, causing the computer to think there is no oil pressure. This is a very common issue that mechanics often overlook, opting to replace the sensor without cleaning the harness.

### 3. Oil Slosh Under Acceleration (Pickup Tube Uncovering)
The 3.5L EcoBoost engine's oil pickup tube is located at the front of the oil pan sump [3]. During acceleration, the oil in the pan sloshes to the rear. If the oil level is even slightly low—even if it still reads "OK" on the dipstick—the pickup tube can become momentarily uncovered, sucking in air instead of oil and triggering the low pressure warning [3]. This is especially common on tuned Raptors or during hard acceleration.

### 4. Clogged VVT Solenoids or Oil Pickup Screen
As engines age (your truck has over 140,000 miles), sludge and debris can build up in the oil pan. This debris can clog the oil pickup tube screen or the Variable Valve Timing (VVT) solenoids [4]. When these screens are clogged, the engine cannot circulate oil fast enough at higher RPMs, leading to a genuine drop in oil pressure.

### 5. Cam Phaser Failure
The 3.5L EcoBoost is notorious for cam phaser issues. Cam phasers rely on engine oil pressure to adjust engine timing. If the phasers are failing, they can cause internal oil pressure bleeds, leading to low pressure warnings [5]. This is usually accompanied by a distinct rattling noise during cold starts.

### 6. Plastic Oil Pan Leak
The 2017-2018 3.5L EcoBoost engines utilize a two-piece plastic oil pan that is highly prone to leaking [6]. While Jiffy Lube said your levels were good, a slow leak from this pan can cause the oil level to drop just enough between checks to cause the "oil slosh" issue mentioned above.

## DIY Fixes and Things to Try at Home

Before taking the truck back to a mechanic and spending hundreds of dollars on diagnostics, there are several steps you can take at home.

| Step | Action | Difficulty | Cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Overfill Oil Slightly** | Add 1/4 to 1/2 quart of oil *above* the full mark on the dipstick. This is a known workaround for the oil slosh/pickup tube issue on the Raptor. If the warning stops appearing during acceleration, you know the pickup tube was being uncovered. | Easy | ~$10 |
| **2. Clean the Sensor Connector** | Locate the oil pressure sensor (near the oil filter housing on the driver's side). Unplug the electrical connector and spray both the sensor and the connector thoroughly with CRC Electronic Cleaner. Let it dry completely and reconnect. | Easy | ~$5 |
| **3. Scan for Hidden Codes** | Use an OBD-II scanner to check for "pending" codes that might not trigger a Check Engine Light. Look specifically for **P0521** (Oil Pressure Sensor Performance) or **P06DD** (Oil Pressure Control Circuit). | Easy | Free (at auto parts store) |
| **4. Replace the Sensor** | If cleaning the connector doesn't work, replace the sensor yourself. The OEM part is Motorcraft CM5Z-9278-A. While manuals say you must remove the turbo pipes, many owners successfully replace it by simply removing the air box and intake chute to access it from the front. | Moderate | ~$30 |
| **5. Inspect for Leaks** | Crawl under the truck and inspect the seam of the plastic oil pan. If it is wet with oil, you have the dreaded plastic pan leak, which may be contributing to the issue. | Easy | Free |

## When to Stop Driving and See a Mechanic

While a faulty sensor is the most likely culprit, driving a vehicle with *actual* low oil pressure will destroy the engine very quickly. **You should stop driving the truck immediately and have it towed to a mechanic if you experience any of the following:**

*   You hear loud ticking, knocking, or metallic rattling noises from the engine.
*   The engine temperature gauge begins to rise above normal.
*   The truck experiences a severe loss of power that does not resolve after restarting.
*   You find metallic glitter or shavings on the dipstick or in the oil.

If you have tried the DIY steps above and the warning persists, ask your mechanic to hook up a **mechanical oil pressure gauge** to the engine. This is the only definitive way to know if the engine actually has low oil pressure, or if the computer is just receiving a bad signal.
