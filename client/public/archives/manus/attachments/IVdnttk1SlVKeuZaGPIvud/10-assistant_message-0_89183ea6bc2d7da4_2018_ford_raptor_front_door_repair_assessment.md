# 2018 Ford Raptor — Front Passenger Door Repair Assessment

**Prepared for ZIP 91403 (Sherman Oaks area)**  
**Vehicle:** 2018 Ford F-150 Raptor  
**Evidence reviewed:** User-supplied passenger-side video

## Bottom line

The visible damage is **not a minor scratch or a paintless-dent-repair job**. The front passenger door’s outer panel appears torn open, with a heavy scrape to bare metal/primer and a visible crease. A reputable repairer should inspect it with the expectation of either replacing the outer skin or, more likely in this situation, replacing the **right-front door shell** and transferring the undamaged components.

For a quality self-pay repair in the 91403 market, I would budget **$4,500–$7,500**, with **approximately $5,500–$6,500** the most realistic working range if the glass, mirror, wiring, latch, hinges, inner door structure, rocker, fender, and running board all prove reusable. This is a planning estimate, not a shop quote. If the inspection finds a damaged inner door, door electronics, hinges, rocker/B-pillar, or an affected adjacent panel, the repair could rise beyond **$8,000**.

> **Recommendation:** Obtain written estimates first from **Regal Repair** and **Ventura Collision Center**, then use **Certified Collision Craft** as the local value comparison. Do not authorize a low quote unless it confirms the repair procedure for the Raptor’s aluminum front door, paint-blend scope, and parts type in writing.

## Likely repair approach

A shop may inspect whether an outer-skin repair is technically viable, but the visible tear makes a **VIN-confirmed right-front door-shell replacement** the more credible baseline. The OEM door-shell benchmark is Ford part **FL3Z-1620124-C**, listed by an online Ford-parts retailer as a front-right shell for 2017–2020 applications. The page lists **$2,256.23 MSRP** and **$1,489.11 discounted**, while correctly cautioning that the VIN must be used to confirm fitment. A door shell is not a complete assembled door; it normally requires transferring compatible glass, trim, handle, latch, wiring, weather seals, and other components. [2]

Ford says its Certified Collision Network centers are equipped to perform proper aluminum repairs using required procedures, including coated boron-steel self-piercing rivets and approved OE paint systems. For this repair, aluminum capability and documented repair procedure matter more than selecting the lowest headline price. [1]

| Work component | What a proper estimate should address |
|---|---|
| Door shell or skin | VIN-confirmed part number; OEM, recycled OEM, or aftermarket status; condition and warranty. |
| Transfer and alignment | Glass, regulator, wiring, handle, latch, trim, mirror, seals, hinges, and operation of the door/window/lock. |
| Aluminum process | Shop’s current Ford/aluminum capability, corrosion protection, adhesive/rivet work where applicable, and repair documentation. |
| Paint | Refinish of the replacement panel; explain whether the fender and/or rear door must be blended for a color match. |
| Diagnostics | Pre- and post-repair scans, plus any calibration or electrical diagnosis actually required. |
| Final quality control | Panel gaps, latch operation, water intrusion check, paint-match inspection, and written warranty. |

## Transparent cost estimate

The calculation below is intentionally conservative and assumes a door-shell replacement route. It includes parts, normal component transfer/reassembly, fasteners/adhesives, paint/refinish work, likely blending, materials, scans, and alignment checks. It does **not** assume hidden damage.

| Scenario | Modeled total | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Lean but proper repair | **$4,149** | Discounted OEM shell, limited adjacent blending, and no hidden issues. Not every certified Los Angeles shop will price at this level. |
| Typical reputable-shop target | **$5,925** | Practical budget for a VIN-confirmed shell, proper aluminum process, normal parts transfer, refinish, and paint match. |
| Higher planning case | **$7,906** | Full MSRP-style part pricing, wider blending, higher labor/material costs, and more diagnostic/alignment allowance—still without hidden damage. |

The figures are a transparent range model, not a promise of a final invoice. A self-pay shop may also offer a lower-cost recycled OEM door option, but color, condition, corrosion protection, trim compatibility, and warranty should be considered before treating that as equivalent to a new OEM shell.

## How to get a fair, apples-to-apples price

Send the same video and the same short request to all three shops. Request a **written self-pay estimate** and ask them to state whether they recommend repair, a replacement skin, or a replacement shell. Ask them to identify the exact part type, include all required paint blending and materials, and disclose whether any supplement could be needed after teardown.

> “I have a 2018 Ford Raptor with a torn front passenger aluminum door outer panel. Please provide a written self-pay estimate. Is your plan repair, door skin, or replacement door shell? Please confirm current Ford/aluminum repair capability, OEM versus recycled/aftermarket parts, paint-blend panels, pre/post scans, hardware/adhesives, and warranty. I want the estimate to include the complete transfer, alignment, and operation check of the glass, latch, locks, and window.”

A lower price is only genuinely comparable if it includes the same repair route, parts source, blend scope, materials, scans, and warranty. If a quote is below about **$4,000**, ask specifically what has been omitted; on the visible damage, that figure could indicate a cosmetic compromise, a recycled part with limited preparation, no blending, or a scope that will later expand.

## Until it is inspected

Do not pull on or attempt to bend the torn metal. Avoid automatic car washes and wet-weather exposure if the puncture is open to the door cavity. If the door does not fully latch, if the window or locks behave abnormally, if there are sharp exposed edges, or if you see loose wiring, arrange towing or a prompt professional inspection rather than treating it as purely cosmetic.
