# Investor Documents Evaluation Report: My Future Capacity + Skills Passport

## Executive Summary

An in-depth review of the **My Future Capacity + Skills Passport** investor materials—comprising the 14-page pitch deck (PDF) and the Investor Bridge Package (Excel)—reveals a compelling narrative around a verified capability infrastructure. The founder’s domain expertise as a former superintendent provides strong credibility, and the problem space (fragmented, unverified skills signaling) is well-articulated. 

However, the current package contains significant structural, financial, and narrative gaps that will likely trigger investor hesitation. The most critical issue is the **material discrepancy between the pitch deck and the financial model**. The deck presents a "Stretch" revenue scenario ($250K to $1.9M) while the Excel model defaults to a "Base Bridge" scenario ($180K to $1.5M), and the Use of Funds breakdowns do not match. Furthermore, the business currently lacks a technical co-founder, signed pilot agreements, and clear unit economics. 

This report outlines specific recommendations on what to remove, edit, and add to ensure the materials are cohesive, defensible, and investor-ready.

---

## 1. Cross-Document Discrepancies (Critical Edits Required)

The most urgent task is to synchronize the data between the pitch deck and the Excel model. Presenting conflicting numbers to investors undermines trust and suggests a lack of financial discipline.

### Revenue Projections Mismatch
The pitch deck (Slide 12) displays a three-year revenue outlook of $250K (Year 1), $850K (Year 2), and $1.9M (Year 3). However, the Excel model's "Selected Case" (Base Bridge) projects $180K, $650K, and $1.5M for the same periods. The Excel model explicitly notes that the deck's numbers represent the "Stretch / original deck path." 
**Recommendation:** Edit Slide 12 of the pitch deck to reflect the Base Bridge scenario ($180K / $650K / $1.5M). If the founder insists on showing the Stretch case, it must be explicitly labeled as such, and the Base case must be presented alongside it.

### Use of Funds Inconsistencies
Slide 13 of the pitch deck outlines a $250,000 Use of Funds that includes a specific line item for "Founder and fractional leadership cash comp" at $60,000 (24%). The Excel model's "Use of Funds" sheet does not include this line item, instead embedding it within "Payroll" and reallocating percentages across other categories (e.g., Product Hardening is 34% in the deck but 28% in the model).
**Recommendation:** Align the Use of Funds categories and percentages perfectly across both documents. It is generally advisable to remove the explicit "Founder cash comp" line from the pitch deck slide and instead use standard categories like "Team & Payroll," as seeing 24% of a small bridge round going directly to founder compensation may raise red flags for early-stage investors.

## 2. Pitch Deck (PDF) Recommendations

The pitch deck is visually strong and tells a good story, but it lacks certain standard elements expected in a seed or bridge round presentation.

### Items to Remove
* **"Planned Outputs" Language:** On Slides 4 and 8, the text mentions "planned outputs include verified PDF..." This contradicts the title of Slide 8 ("Built, Tested, and Entering the Market"). Remove the word "planned" and state what the MVP currently does.
* **Garbled Characters in Competitor Table:** Slide 10 contains rendering errors where "X" marks appear as garbled boxes. Remove these and replace them with clean, standard checkmarks and X symbols.

### Items to Edit
* **Market Sizing (Slide 6):** The slide presents TAM, SAM, and SOM primarily in terms of learner count, but only provides dollar figures for TAM and SAM. The SOM dollar figure ($1.275M) is buried in the text. Edit this slide to prominently display the dollar values for all three tiers, as investors think in revenue potential, not just user counts.
* **Team Slide (Slide 11):** The current slide highlights a solo founder and lists three "Next Key Hire" positions. Edit this to include any current advisors, fractional executives, or development agencies building the MVP. A solo non-technical founder building an AI infrastructure platform is a major risk; showing who is actually writing the code today is essential.
* **The Ask (Slide 13):** The investment instrument is listed as "Open to SAFE, equity, pilot sponsorship..." Edit this to state a specific primary instrument (e.g., "Raising $250K on a standard Post-Money SAFE"). Offering too much flexibility can signal a lack of conviction or lead to complex, non-standard terms.

### Items to Add
* **Traction / Pipeline Slide:** Slide 8 mentions "Active conversations" and "discussions in progress." Add a slide or a section detailing the actual pipeline: How many institutions? What is the total contract value (TCV) of the pipeline? Are there any signed Letters of Intent (LOIs) or Memorandums of Understanding (MOUs)?
* **Unit Economics / Pricing Model:** Slide 7 mentions a $15/learner annual fee but states "Commercial pricing will be finalized by pilot scope." Add clarity on the expected Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and Lifetime Value (LTV), or at least the target margins, to prove the business model is viable.
* **Contact Information:** Add the founder's email address, phone number, and a link to the data room on the final slide (Slide 14). Currently, contact info only appears on the cover slide.

## 3. Investor Bridge Package (Excel) Recommendations

The Excel model is well-structured, logically sound, and includes excellent internal checks. However, it contains internal notes that should not be shared with external parties.

### Items to Remove
* **Self-Audit Sheet:** Sheet 8 ("Self-Audit Against Delivery Standard") is an internal checklist that explicitly states the package is "PASS WITH ONE EVIDENCE CONDITION" because it lacks live CRM/LOI evidence. Remove this sheet entirely before sending it to investors, as it highlights weaknesses and is meant for internal preparation.
* **Investor Q&A Sheet:** Sheet 9 ("Likely Funder Questions and Planned Answers") is a great preparation tool for the founder but should not be included in the external Excel file. Remove it and keep it as a separate internal reference document.

### Items to Edit
* **Month 1 Cash Balance:** The "Monthly Cash Flow" sheet shows a beginning cash balance of $0. If the company has any existing capital, grant money, or founder investment, edit this to reflect the actual starting bank balance.
* **Payroll Ramp-Up:** The cash flow model shows payroll jumping from ~$8.3K/month to ~$24K/month in Month 13. Edit the assumptions or add a "Headcount" tab to explicitly show who is being hired at Month 13 to justify this 3x increase in burn rate.

### Items to Add
* **Headcount Roster:** Add a simple tab detailing the hiring plan. Who is the $8.3K/month paying for in Year 1? When do the "Next Key Hires" from the pitch deck join, and at what salaries?
* **Pipeline / CRM Tab:** Add a tab listing the 2-3 anchor institutional partners mentioned in the deck. Include their names (or anonymized descriptions), stage of negotiation, expected pilot start date, and potential contract value. This addresses the "evidence condition" flagged in the internal audit.

---

## Conclusion

The My Future Capacity + Skills Passport narrative is strong, and the $250K bridge ask is reasonable for the stated milestones. However, the package currently feels like a draft that has not been fully synchronized. By aligning the financial figures across both documents, removing internal audit notes, clarifying the technical team structure, and firming up the pipeline evidence, the founder will present a much more cohesive, professional, and investable opportunity.
