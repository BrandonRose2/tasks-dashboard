# Extracted Requirements from Recording

## Scope clarification
The relevant discussion appears to concern **resident applicant screening and leasing decisions**, rather than hiring property-management employees. The audio includes unrelated training/presentation content, but the core discussion runs roughly from 02:32 to 11:07.

## Stated operational problem
Site teams are pressured to fill vacancies quickly. This can result in residents being approved without meaningful verification, followed by severe early delinquency, eviction costs, turn costs, and additional vacancy time. Leslie’s view is that simply increasing a credit-score cutoff will not solve the problem.

## Desired decision factors
- Rental history and prior rent-payment performance
- Current household income, generally 2.5x–3.0x rent
- Current-job tenure
- Prior employment history / job continuity
- Previous landlord verification
- Credit information as a limited first-pass input, not the primary determinant
- Subsidy/voucher, Section 8, LIHTC, HUD, or conventional-property context

## Desired workflow controls
- Property-specific, transparent, configurable scoring variables (e.g., score bands for 6 months and 12 months of tenure)
- Required checkboxes and documentation for verification steps
- An auditable record showing whether staff actually completed rental and employment verification
- A way to prevent unsupported or invented verification records, especially at HUD properties and during MOR audits
- Manager guidance that makes the cost of a poor approval visible relative to the cost of a short vacancy
- Simple enough experience that managers will use it under time pressure

## Related precedent
A prior system pulled job applications from Indeed and scored candidates using relevant experience, education, LIHTC experience, and OneSite experience, then surfaced the strongest 25–40 candidates out of roughly 1,500. The concept was workable but not adopted, suggesting usability, workflow fit, and ownership are critical to adoption.

## Important implementation constraint
Any resident-screening recommendation must be configurable by property type and jurisdiction, use consistently applied written criteria, include adverse-action/documentation controls where appropriate, and be reviewed by qualified housing counsel/compliance staff before use.
